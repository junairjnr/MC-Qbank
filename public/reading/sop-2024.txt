Coffee History and Culture
INTRODUCTION TO 
COFFEE
Did you know:
The tradition of starting each day with a cup of coffee has been an obsession in different parts 
of the  world for centuries.
• Overone billion cups of coffee are consumed each day across the world.
• Coffee is one of the world's most valuable commodities, ranking second to oil in terms of 
dollars traded on internationalmarkets.
• Coffee is grown in approximately 70 countries. The highest 3 producers are Brazil, Vietnam 
and Colombia.
Our aim is to teach you the art of making espresso coffee and how to deliver the perfect cup 
every time. As every coffee lover knows, it is not just the quality of the beans used but also 
the skill of the barista that produces the best espresso. We have blended the finestcoffee 
beans...the rest is up to you.
BRIEF HISTORY OF COFFEE
Legends abound regarding the origins of coffee. No one knows for sure exactly how coffee was discovered 
but there are several theories. One of the more popular legends refers to a goat herder named Kaldi, in the 
area of
Ethiopia, Africa, who, in around 600AD, observed the goats acting unusually frisky after eating berries from a 
bush. Curious about this phenomenon Kaldi tried eating the berries himself. 
He found that these berries gave him a renewed energy. The news of this energy-laden fruit quickly spread 
throughout the region.
Monks, hearing about this amazing fruit, dried the berries so that they could be transported to distant 
monasteries. They reconstituted these berries in water, ate the fruit, anddrank the liquid to provide 
stimulation for a more
awakened time of prayer.
The coffee plant is native to Africa. It was known that coffee berries were eaten whole at first or crushed and 
mixed with fat and carried by wandering tribesman northwards to the Arabian region. Here the coffee then 
traveled to Turkeywhere the Dutch orchestrated the first successful planting outside Arabia -on the island of 
Java- in 1699.
The coffee plant and the start of modern d a ycafes (known as coffee houses) spreadthroughout Europe and 
The Americas in the 17th and 18th century
COFFEE ROASTING
• The Arabian people were the first to roast coffee beans over an open fire. They then ground the 
beans into a powder, to be mixed at a later time with water - creating what is now known as a ‘cup of 
coffee'. Atradition had been started!
• The roasting process and equipment has been perfected through the centuries to provide a more 
consistent product with optimal flavour characteristics.
• Roasting involves heating the green (raw) coffee beans in a cylindrical drum for approximately 12-15 
minutes at high temperatures, which can exceed 200°C.
• As the beans cook, they change colour to become brown, lose moisture and become more brittle. 
During this process the wonderful flavours and aromas that we know as coffee are created.
•The roaster must carefully control the roasting process to ensure the desired roast (i.e. darkness 
ofthe coffee) is achieved.
•Once the roasting is complete, the beans are cooled before packing ready for distribution.

Coffee History and Culture
BOTANY OF THE BEEN
There are two major species that are grown for commercial use: coffeaarabica (known as 'Arabica') and coffea 
canepholtanown as 'Robusta’).
Left to mature, the coffee plant wouldgrow to a height of 10
metres, but on plantations the plants are pruned to more easily
allow harvesting.
It takes 3-5 years for the plant to produce its first fruit and each
tree annually produces enough coffee forapproximately 40 cups of coffee.
Properly lookedafter, a coffee plant can be productive for up to 50 years.
Arabica:
Grown at higher altitudes (up to 2000 metres above sea level)
where there is sufficient rainfall. Typically the higher the altitude, the
better the quality, it is richer in flavour and has ahigher acidity level, with an excellentaroma.
Robusta:
Flourishes at lower altitudes (up to 800 metres above sea level), is disease resistant and quicker growing. 
Contains more caffeine, less acidity and is generally of a lower quality, hence thereason for using it in 
instantcoffee. It is also used in espresso blends where more body and crema is required.The Robusta bean 
tends to be smaller and more rounded in shape.
DECAFFEINATED 
COFFEE
Decaffeination is the name given to the process wherecaffeine is removedfrom the green (raw) 
beans. Roasted coffee beansmusthave less than 0.1% caffeineto becalled 'decaffeinated".

LANGUAUGE OF COFFEE
Fragrance A key word describing the freshness and quality of coffee is 'fragrance'- which is released when the bean is roasted and then again when 
it's ground
Aroma When hot water hits theground coffee, tasters refer to its 'aroma. Words used to describe the aroma include delicate, moderate, strong, rich, 
fragrant or complex
Body More experienced tasters can discuss the 'body' of a coffes - the way a coffee is experienced by the mouth. A full-bodied coffee has a rich 
texture and heaviness on the tongue, with a taste that lingers. Coffee lacking body is thin and watery.
Crema Espresso coffee should have a golden coloured layer called 'crema'. Crema looks a little like honey,
but is in fact a product of the oil in the beans. The crema makes a 'cap' which helps retain the aroma and flavours of the espresso within the cup .The 
presence of crema indicates quality
Acidity The word 'acidity which is often used to describe coffee, refers to the pleasingly crisp, sharp tasteof coffee
Bite Roasting eliminates some of the acidity, so a light roasted coffee will contain the highest amount and is said to have a lot of 'bite.
Flavour is the combination of the aroma and the taste that thecoffee impresses in the mouth. There are a large number of terms relating to 
flavour,such as nutty, caramelly, spicy, fruity, smoky, chocolaty, bland and herby
The exact 'flavour’ of each coffee bean is influenced not only by roasting but also by where it's grown. Key factors include the type of soil the plant is 
grown in, the climate, the way the plants are cultivated and the method of picking

McCafe Basics
Milk Frothing
1. Fill milk jug
• Choose the correct size jug.
• Attach a thermometer to the jug and fill about 1/3 full of cold milk.
• Do not heat  more than the amount of milk required
• Return the milk bottle to the refrigerator.
2. Purge the Steam Wand
• Quickly release steam for a few seconds by turning steam on and off whilst 
directing stream wand into drip tray using a Green cloth.
3. Insert Steam Wand
• Place milk jug with a thermometer under the steam nozzle and immerse 
the nozzle as far as possible without the nozzle actually touching the base 
of the jug.
• Hold just with the handle towards you and place the steam arm to pull 
against the jug's spout.
• Open the steam valve by pressing the steam button
• Immediately place hand on the base of the jug for support and to sense 
temperature
4. Froth Milk
• Lower jug immediately so nozzle is 5-10 mm below surface to aerate milk.
• After a few seconds, gently lower jug down still ensuring nozzle is 
submerged just below milk surface. It should make a hissing sound.
• If there is no sound, the nozzle is too deep and will not froth milk. If too far 
out milk will spit and could burn.
• Continue lowering jug as froth rises until the milk is 40°C and keep the jug 
steady until temperature reach 60°C 
• Prevent burns - only touch steam wand by black rubber grip or with cloth
5. Monitor Temperature
• Stop heating milk when thermometer reaches 60°C 
• If froth has reached top of jug and temperature is not at 60°C, lower nozzle 
into jug to heat milk further.
• Tap jug gently on bench to release large bubbles.
• It is important to maintain milk temperature between 60°C and 70°C. The 
best indicator to assist in knowing when to remove jug is when it just 
becomes too hot to touch the base.
6. Clean steam wand
• After every use, purge steam wand for 3 secs, ensure steam valve is off and 
wipe steam nozzle and steam wand clean with correct (green check) clean, 
sanitized cloth. Clothes are not to be mixed.
• If this fails to remove build up, wrap a cloth around the steam arm, turn steam 
on for 10 secs and keep cloth in place.
• Remove the cloth carefully.
• Be careful not to bum yourself on the hot steam wand.

until it’s  completely dispensed.
GRINDER CLEANING PROCEDURE
